Arrange: big room

Writes a complete big-room house arrangement into a new project — intro 8 · build 16 · drop 32 · break 16 · build 16 · drop 32 · outro 8, 128 bars, four minutes at 128 — as ordinary tracks, clips and notes you can edit afterwards. Kick (tuned to the key, so it sits on the sub's root instead of beating against it), sub on the kick's rests, and THE HOOK: an 8-bar lead written from the seed in the big-room shape — a 2-4 note cell with an octave leap, call then response, every note on the "and" so the kick's pump reads as a swell, ending on the root; the second drop plays a variant. The shape is the reference, the melody is original — the arranger never reproduces a song. Clap and open hat, a snare roll and a riser into each drop, a crash and a sub-drop on each downbeat. The lead and sub are sidechained from the kick with a measured 3/16-beat release (88 ms at 128: back within 2 dB by the next "and"); the master aims at −8 LUFS under a −1 dBTP limiter. Same seed, same song.

Latency calibration

Recording arrives late by however long your speakers → air → microphone → driver round trip takes. This wizard measures that once per input device and the recorder then places every take earlier by exactly that offset, so what you played on the beat sits on the beat.

It needs your speakers audible to your microphone: turn the volume to a normal level, keep the room quiet, and don't use headphones — the mic must hear the chirp.

–

Honesty: the synthetic path proves the estimator and the storage end-to-end (±1 ms on a known offset); only a real mic run proves your hardware.

Track colour

Colour is orientation: the same hue marks the track's head, its clips, its notes and its mixer strip. The default is derived from the track's id, so an agent that adds a track gets a colour without being told one.
