# THE QUIET — treatment and shot list

A short film. Target 5 minutes, ~60 shots at ~5 s. Sci-fi, oceanic, no dialogue
that needs lip-sync.

---

## The idea, and why this one

The obvious Baltic Sea Anomaly film is: something is down there, we wake it, it
attacks the ship. That story is finished before it starts — the audience knows
the shape of it from the logline.

**Inversion: the anomaly has been making a sound for a hundred and forty thousand
years, and the excavation is what makes it stop.** The horror is not something
waking up. It is something going quiet, and the dawning understanding of what the
sound was *for*.

The second turn: it is not a craft. It is a **marker**. Ocean floors across the
planet have them, spaced like the stones around a field — and the sound was not a
signal to the stars. It was a fence. Something is being kept on the correct side
of it.

So the battleship's panic is not "a monster surfaced." It is sonar operators
watching a boundary they never knew existed stop being enforced, and every
instrument on the ship agreeing at once.

The last shot is the only cheap thrill in it, and it is earned: the camera pulls
back off the North Atlantic and there are *other* quiet places, all over the map.

## Tone

Cold, procedural, grey-blue. Closer to a salvage documentary than a monster
picture — steel, sodium lamps, condensation, sonar green. The alien element is
never fully seen. What is seen is instrumentation and the faces of people reading
it, which is also convenient: gauges and faces at close range are the two things
this pipeline renders best.

## Structure — five movements

| # | movement | shots | what happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **The Sound** | 1–10 | 1997 archival texture. A hydrophone array picks up a 22-second repeating pulse. Nobody can place it. It is catalogued and forgotten. |
| II | **The Lift** | 11–26 | Present day. A salvage vessel over the Baltic. ROV descends. The object: sixty metres across, too regular, mineral-crusted, with a machined seam. They rig it. |
| III | **The Silence** | 27–40 | The lift succeeds. The pulse — which nobody on the ship knew they had been hearing — stops. Ninety seconds of a crew realising the ocean has changed pitch. |
| IV | **The Answer** | 41–54 | A destroyer on station goes to action. Sonar fills with returns that are not vessels. The ocean is answering from below the thermocline, and the returns are converging on the hole where the marker used to be. |
| V | **The Map** | 55–60 | Silence. The chart room. A researcher marks the site, then marks the others — the Mariana site, the Puerto Rico site, the Weddell site. Pull back. There are hundreds. |

## Shot list

Beats are ~5 s each. Written as prompt seeds — content and camera, no dialogue.

**I. THE SOUND (1–10)**
1. Black. A waveform crawls across an oscilloscope, green on black, one repeating spike.
2. CU reel-to-reel tape, 1997, spooling. Dust in a sodium desk lamp.
3. Hands adjusting a rack-mount filter, headphones on a hook, cigarette smoke.
4. Wide: a listening station at night, one window lit in a concrete building.
5. CU a paper chart recorder scratching an identical pulse, over and over.
6. A technician's face lit from below by the scope, listening, unimpressed.
7. Insert: a manila folder stamped UNIDENTIFIED — BIOLOGIC? and a date.
8. The folder goes into a drawer among a thousand identical folders.
9. Slow push on the drawer closing.
10. Cut to black. The pulse continues over the black, alone.

**II. THE LIFT (11–26)**
11. Aerial: a salvage vessel alone on flat grey water, dawn, no land anywhere.
12. Deck level: crane, A-frame, coiled umbilical, crew in orange.
13. ROV lowered past the rail, camera on its dome, sea closing over the lens.
14. Descent POV: particulate streaming up past the lights, blue going to black.
15. Sonar screen: a shape resolving out of the seabed noise, too regular.
16. ROV lights sweep across a mineral-crusted surface — a curved edge, a shadow.
17. Wide underwater: the object, sixty metres, half-buried, lit in three cones.
18. CU: a seam. Machined. Straight for as far as the light reaches.
19. The ROV arm brushing sediment away. A grid of shallow depressions beneath.
20. Ship's lab: the ROV feed on a monitor, three faces watching, no one speaking.
21. Sonar operator's face, headphones, brow tightening at something in the audio.
22. Insert: spectrogram, a 22-second period repeating, clean as a heartbeat.
23. Divers rigging strops in floodlit black water, silt rolling.
24. Winch drum turning. Steel cable coming up taut and shedding water.
25. The object breaking the seabed — an enormous slow shrug of sediment.
26. Surface: the crane taking the weight, the whole ship listing a few degrees.

**III. THE SILENCE (27–40)**
27. CU: the spectrogram. The pulse stops mid-period. A flat green line.
28. The sonar operator pulls one headphone off. Looks at it. Puts it back.
29. Wide of the lab. Everyone has stopped moving. Nobody knows why yet.
30. Deck: the object clearing the water, streaming, enormous, wrong.
31. CU on its surface as water sheets off — the depressions are a script.
32. A gull banking away hard from the ship, then another, then all of them.
33. Underwater: the hole where it was. Sediment settling. Nothing else.
34. Ship's clock. Ambient hydrophone gain being turned up, and up, and up.
35. Nothing on it. Not quiet — EMPTY. A crew member notices that first.
36. CU the captain's face, listening to an absence.
37. Wide aerial: the vessel, tiny, on water that has gone glassy.
38. The thermocline on a display: a warm layer, and below it, movement.
39. Someone very quietly asks for the fathometer to be turned back on.
40. Fathometer. The bottom is not where it was.

**IV. THE ANSWER (41–54)**
41. A destroyer, grey, cutting hard toward station at speed.
42. CIC interior, red light, faces lit by scopes.
43. Sonar: three returns below the layer. Then eleven. Then too many to plot.
44. An operator's hand hesitating over a switch.
45. General quarters. Bodies moving fast down a narrow passage, hatches dogging.
46. Deck: a mount training out over an empty sea. Nothing to aim at.
47. Bridge: binoculars, horizon, flat water, no wake, no fin, nothing.
48. Sonar again: the returns are not approaching the ship. They are converging on the salvage site.
49. Salvage vessel: crew at the rail looking down at water that is doming.
50. Underwater: the empty excavation. Something moving at the edge of the lights, out of focus, wrong-scaled.
51. Instruments failing over — compass spinning, depth reading nonsense.
52. CU face, sodium light, the exact moment of understanding.
53. Cable parting. The object going back down, dragging the crane with it.
54. Underwater wide: it settling into the hole. Precisely. Like a lid.

**V. THE MAP (55–60)**
55. Black. Then — the pulse resumes. One spike. Then the period.
56. CIC: every return dropping off the display at once. Empty ocean again.
57. Chart room, later. A researcher pins the Baltic site.
58. She pins another. And another. Mariana. Puerto Rico. Weddell.
59. Pull back off the chart: hundreds of pins, evenly spaced, worldwide.
60. Final: the map fills frame, and the spacing reads unmistakably as a fence.

## Production notes

- **Engine: LTX 2.5.** No lip-sync required, and it is 5.5× faster than H3 with
  better results by eye. ~121 s per clip at 1280×704 × 121 frames, so 60 shots is
  roughly two hours of GPU.
- **Camera moves come from the prompt, not from blocking.** The gate settled this:
  sparse appearance guides cannot carry a blocked camera without also handing back
  the blockout (`gate_report.json`, DECISIVE NEGATIVE). Blender previz is still
  worth rendering for *shot planning* and for props, but it does not drive the
  generator on this path.
- **Props via Blender → reference images.** The marker object, the ROV, and the
  instrument faces are worth rendering in `C:/temp/AIPLAYStudio-blender` and
  feeding as reference images. That path works — the bleed finding proves
  references work *strongly*.
- **Watch the opening frames of any reference-conditioned shot.** Per
  `docs/H3_REFERENCE_BLEED.md`, at 4 steps a reference can occupy the first ~10
  frames almost verbatim. If any shot uses H3 with references, trim the head or
  cut into the shot late.
- **The alien is never shown in focus.** Partly a tone decision, mostly an honest
  one: out-of-focus mass at the edge of a light cone is something this pipeline
  renders convincingly, and a clearly-shown creature is not.
